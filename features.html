features

Everything Hawk does, and just as important, everything it refuses to do.

the basics

  • tabs: Ctrl+T open, Alt+W close, Ctrl+L bar, Ctrl+R reload
  • pill-shaped omnibar and pill tabs, nothing else in the way
  • popups become tabs, because popups are for cowards
  • file downloads land without ceremony

it remembers (on purpose)

  • URL memory — every page you visit is kept in ~/.hawk/history and offered back as you type. start typing and it finishes for you, like any browser should have from year one
  • sessions — your open tabs are saved on close and restored on launch. hit the power and it doesn't forget what you were doing
  • cookies that stay — stored in ~/.hawk/cookies/cookies.sqlite. log in once, stay logged in. the old version logged you out every single time you closed it, which was the single most depressing bug in it

it forgets (on purpose)

  • third-party cookies blocked by default
  • camera & microphone off until you say otherwise (deep breath — you can't say otherwise yet, visit hawk://config ... also no. it's locked. sorry)
  • one fixed user agent, no fingerprint breadcrumbs from Hawk itself
  • no telemetry, no account, no sync-to-nowhere, no "AI" anything

the search part

Hawk runs its own SearXNG, bound to 127.0.0.1:8080, on your machine. It starts with the browser, stops with it, and your queries never leave home. If it can't start, Hawk falls back to DuckDuckGo HTML rather than silently routing you somewhere heavier.

what it is not

  • not a Chrome reskin
  • not a data collector
  • not a Firefox — it cannot run Mozilla / AMO add-ons, and it will never pretend otherwise
  • not available for Windows or macOS, and never will be
  • not finished
